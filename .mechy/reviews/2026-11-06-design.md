## Blocking

1. **Gripper plate (gripper base, crank gears A/B, 2 idler links, 2 jaws): the parts are built on a servo horn that sits
   3.2 mm lower than the cited drawing shows.**
   - Evidence: `inspect parts/models/mg996r-horn.step` gives a bbox of z 2.3..7.8 above the case top. The drawing in the
     cited PDF shows 36.6 mm for the body and 47.6 mm overall with the horn, so the horn top is 11.0 mm above the case
     top. The spec text gives "40.7 x 19.7 x 42.9", which is the case plus the spline only.
   - Crank A sits on the horn: geometry.py:104-105 sets `FINGER_Y0 = GRIP_HORN_Y[1]` = 0.8. Crank B, the idler links and
     the base bosses are placed from the gripper base instead (gripper_base.py:33-34, idler_link.py).
   - With the drawing value, crank A rises 3.2 mm. Its teeth would span y 4.0..10.0 against crank B's 1.4..6.8, which
     leaves 2.8 mm of the 5.4 mm face in mesh, and jaw A would sit 3.2 mm higher on J1 than on J2.
   - The horn is not in mg996r-servo.yaml critical_dims. summary.md:148-153 and gcode/README.md:28,34 already hold this
     plate, but its G-code exists and it is in the print order.
   - The same parts have more problems to fix when you regenerate them:
     - **Crank A horn-screw counterbore** is Ø3.8 x 2.0 (crank_gear.py:45). §2 asks for 4.4 x 2.3 for M2, and an M2
       head is 3.8. The wall from that counterbore to the gear root is 1.22 mm (printcheck min_wall 1.22; 10.125 − 7 −
       1.9), against 2.0 in §4. The horn screws are not a BOM item.
     - **J1-A pin** (M3 x 20) ends at y −2.0, while its nut spans y −1.6..0.8. That is 0.4 mm past the nut; §2 asks for
       one pitch (0.5).
     - **Servo tab screws** are not in the assembly. An M3 x 10 through the 2.5 mm tab into the 5 mm plate pilot
       (gripper_base.py:57) engages 5 mm; §2 asks for 2.5 x d = 7.5 mm when self-tapping.
     - **Servo window** is 19.7 + 0.8 = 20.5 mm wide (gripper_base.py:24, CLR 0.4). §1 asks for 19.7 + 2 x (0.3 + 0.3)
       = 20.9, and the drawing's end view is labelled 20.
     - **Loose pivot nuts.** Seven running pivots rely on plain nuts left loose with "thread glue" (summary.md:126,
       156-157). The gear-B and idler nuts sit 0.2 mm above their parts in every pose. Neither threadlocker nor nyloc
       nuts are in components.yaml.
   - Fix: keep the plate on hold. Measure the horn top above the case top, the horn screw circle, the tab holes and the
     case-top-to-tab distance. Add them to mg996r-servo.yaml, regenerate, re-run check and the poses, then re-slice.

## Should fix

1. **There is no cable route from the base to the turret, and the turret sweeps low over the controller.**
   - The Uno + CNC-shield box tops out at z 54 (posts z 9 + model height 45, `inspect`). The right tower tail (deck
     underside z 62.5, out to x −126.8) and the shoulder motor (z 90.7 and up) swing over the board through the
     ±150° yaw range. That leaves 8.5 mm for the shield's motor plugs and cable bends.
   - Four cables (shoulder, elbow, wrist, servo) and the yaw switch lead have to get from the fixed board to the
     turret. The base boss is solid and the deck has only the Ø8.4 bolt opening, so there is no routing feature.
   - Missing keep-outs:
     - the HEM wrist motor's rear connector (hem-17d2008-10.yaml:23-24 says "no keep-out modelled"; this motor meets
       the shoulder motor near the q2 +100 limit, geometry.py:18-20);
     - the shield headers;
     - the servo lead;
     - hex-key access above the screws (assembly skill, step 5).
   - Fix: decide the route before printing the base and turret. For example, a free loop outside the sweep radius
     (about 131 mm), or a channel through the yaw axis. Then add the keep-outs and re-check.

2. **The turret slot roofs are 70° overhangs, not the flat bridges summary.md:122 describes.**
   - Measured faces (normal Z −0.94, printed deck down, no supports in the plate):
     - shoulder motor pilot slot roof: 54 mm², 5.6 mm wide, z 64.6..66.7;
     - M3 slot roofs: 8 × 33 mm² and counterbore roofs 4 × 21 mm² at z 35-39 and 66-70;
     - I-axle slot and counterbore roofs: 17.5 / 27.5 / 22 / 23 mm².
   - The S-axle M4 nut trap roof is 60° (2 × 14.7 mm²).
   - The slots follow u_m and u_i, 20° from horizontal (geometry.py:60, I_DIR 200 / M_DIR 160), so each roof steps
     about 0.55 mm per 0.2 mm layer.
   - The Ø23 pilot slot has only 0.5 mm per side for the Ø22 pilot, so roof droop can stop the motor seating.
   - Fix: give the slot roofs a 45° or flat top in print Z, or plan to clean them with a file and a Ø3.5 / Ø4.3 drill.
     Correct the summary either way.

3. **PLA parts touch stepper faces, and PLA carries the axle clamp loads (fdm_pla_rules §8).**
   - The turret (right tower), both shanks and the yaw motor plate touch motor faces.
     assumptions/motor-mount-material.yaml:6 estimates the cases at about 75 °C at 1.0 A; PLA softens at 55-60 °C.
   - The 8 PLA race washers (1.0 mm) and the PLA spacer tubes are clamped by every axle nut, and PLA creeps under that
     preload.
   - Plate 2 (turret and both shanks) takes 9 h 06 m. Printing those three parts and the yaw plate in PETG now is
     cheaper than reprinting them after the 10-minute temperature test.

4. **allow_contact.**
   - The 137 pairs in assembly.yaml:1546-1822 have no reason written in the file.
   - I measured every pair at home:
     - No hidden overlap except where it is designed: belts on teeth (85-243 mm³), screws in motor and pilot threads
       (5.1 / 7.6 / 14.3 / 16.8 mm³), and the horn on its spline (3.6 mm³).
     - The yaw switch overlaps its base pocket by 0.16 mm³.
     - Three pairs never touch: yaw-bolt/axk2035 (8.05 mm), wrist-axle/gripper-base (3.05 mm) and wrist-nut/gripper-base
       (1.12 mm).
   - Ten whole-part pairs mute the check in all 16 pose files, though each gap measures 0 to 0.5 mm. Examples:
     jaw/crank and jaw/idler at 0.5 mm, crank-gear-b/gripper-base at 0.5 mm, gripper-base/wrist-pulley at 0, and
     motor/mount pairs at 0.
   - Fix: drop the pairs that never touch and write a reason for each remaining pair.

5. **The wrist 60T has no flange on its +y side.** wrist_pulley.py:20-22 puts a flange (r 21) only on the gripper
   side. The teeth (y −7.2..−1.0) face the upper shank, whose outline at W is only r 13 (upper_shank.py:27), against a
   tooth radius of 18.8. Nothing stops the belt walking off toward +y.

6. **Walls under 2.0 mm (§4) next to threads.**
   - Wrist pulley: the Ø2.6 self-tap pilots at r 15 come within 1.8 mm of the tooth grooves (18.095 − 16.3) for z
     9.8..12, which is where the M3 x 16 tips thread in.
   - Yaw pulley: the M3 counterbore (r 35.25) comes within 1.94 mm of the 120T groove bottom (37.19) for z 5.7..10.6
     (printcheck min_wall 1.94).
   - Fix: pilots at r 14 or 9.5 mm deep; counterbore circle at r 31.5.

7. **The yaw motor screws engage 3 mm.** An M3 x 6 under the 3 mm plate (yaw_motor_plate.py:5) overlaps the motor
   thread by 5.089 mm³ (3.0 mm); §2 asks for 1.5 x d = 4.5 mm. The motor stands on the table (body z 0..40), so the
   load is small. Write the deviation down, or use a 2 mm plate with an M3 x 6 (4 mm engaged, within the 4.5 mm
   modelled thread depth).

8. **Motor pulley set screws can only be reached before the motor is mounted, and the assembly order is not written.**
   - The hubs (z 0..7) sit inside the mounts: the shoulder hub at y 31.5..24.5 inside the right tower (25..34), the
     elbow at −14.6..−7.6 inside the lower shank plate, and the wrist at 7.4..0.4 inside the upper shank. The set
     screws at hub z 3.5 cannot be reached in place.
   - The belt planes depend on each hub sitting 0.5 / 1.4 / 0.6 mm from the pilot, and those gaps come from estimated
     pulley dimensions (see Unverified 1).
   - Fix: write a pulley-setting step (a spacer gauge) into the assembly order.

9. **Two bought models have no yaml.** parts/models has no arduino-uno-r3.yaml or xl4015-buck.yaml. The Uno post
   pattern in base.py:32 matches the Arduino pattern (13.97/2.54, 15.24/50.8, 66.04/7.62, 66.04/35.56 mm). The buck
   posts in base.py:33 use a 45 x 20 mm diagonal pattern with no source. Those posts are printed into the base.

10. **The nut models are undersized.** fasteners.py:30 builds the nuts from e_min/2 + 0.05. `inspect` gives M3 nuts
    5.29 mm across flats and M4 nuts 6.72 mm, against ISO 4032 5.5 and 7.0 (standard_parts/fasteners_hex_nuts.csv). That
    makes the nut-near-moving-part and trap clearances in `check` about 0.1-0.14 mm per side optimistic.

11. **Looks and legibility (legible_cad §5).**
    - No printed part imports style.py: radii are literals (6.0 / 3.0 / 8.0), and neither the C_BED 0.4 bed chamfer nor
      R_EDGE on touched edges is applied (jaw pads, link ends).
    - style.py:1 still says "v2".
    - The idler links use `#222222` (the servo/prop role colour), and the jaws use `#E8A33D`, which is not in COLORS.
    - All plates are sliced with one Generic PLA (`#00AE42` in slice_info), while plate 1 mixes ivory parts (base,
      plate, spacers) with orange accent parts (yaw pulley, compound, wrist pulley). Either colour scheme needs re-plating.
    - The 11 mm disc windows on both shanks fall inside the 10.2-35.6 mm finger-trap band on rotating discs.
    - The STL and print_list row are named `idler-link-a` (×2) for a part whose source is idler_link.py and which is
      used as both -a and -b. Mirrored pairs use -a/-b, not -left/-right.

    - geometry.py:113 says "y -1.1 .. 4.3"; the value is 1.4..6.8.

   turret sweep (Should fix 1).
